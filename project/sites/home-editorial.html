Areas of Focus

Six disciplines, one practice — in the stewardship of public resources.

Each line below reflects a body of work, not a label. They share a common conviction: that public money, made legible, becomes accountable.
i.

Public Finance

The economics of how governments raise, allocate, and account for public resources — from local treasuries to national systems.

ii.

Government Budgeting

Designing, executing, and explaining budgets that translate political intent into operational reality.

iii.

Fiscal Modernization

Re-engineering the practices, systems, and culture of public-sector finance for clarity, integrity, and resilience.

iv.

Transparency & Accountability

Making the workings of public money visible — so that scrutiny becomes possible and trust becomes earned.

v.

Civic Financial Education

Bringing the language of public finance into civic life — so citizens, students, and community leaders can read it for themselves.

vi.

Local Government Finance

The municipal scale — where budgets meet streets, schools, and clinics — and where most public money lives in plain sight.

Featured Essay

What Is a City Budget — and Why Should You Care?

How Public Money Works Civic Education 8 min read
Every year, your city decides how to spend hundreds of millions of dollars — on streets, schools, libraries, parks, fire trucks, water lines. That decision is called a budget. It is not a technical document for accountants. It is a public statement of priorities, written in numbers. This essay walks through how a city budget is built, who has a voice in it, and what a citizen can actually see by reading one.

About

A career spent reading budgets aloud, so that more people can.

For more than a decade, Eliana Faria Lima has worked at the intersection of public finance, government budgeting, and health governance — designing fiscal systems, writing on accountability, and translating the language of public money into something a citizen can read.

Her practice spans local administrations, public-sector reform, and civic education. Her current work focuses on bringing American public-finance literacy into reach of the people it most affects: residents, small-business owners, students, and community leaders who live with the consequences of how budgets are written.
