## Editorial angle

```yaml
coreQuestion: "When a resident pays a local tax, how can they understand which government receives it, what restrictions apply, and how it may support public services?"
uniqueAngle: "Follow the revenue pathway instead of promising a simple tax-to-service map: identify the payer-facing tax, the collecting and receiving government, the relevant fund or restriction, and the service connection shown in official documents."
whatThisArticleWillNotCover:
  - "Personal tax advice or instructions for challenging an individual tax bill."
  - "A national ranking of tax burdens, cities, states, or public services."
  - "A claim that one tax payment directly pays for one specific service."
  - "State-specific tax law, exemptions, assessment appeals, or filing deadlines without a separately approved jurisdictional scope."
  - "Political judgments about whether a tax level or spending choice is good or bad."
jurisdictionScope: "United States, with clearly labeled examples from one or more local jurisdictions. Explain variation among cities, counties, school districts, and special districts; avoid presenting any single structure as universal."
examplesAllowed:
  - "A simplified diagram or table showing the documented path of a revenue source in a named jurisdiction."
  - "A budget-page example distinguishing tax revenue from fees, fines, grants, transfers, and other sources."
  - "A practical checklist of questions a resident can ask while reading an official budget or revenue document."
examplesToAvoid:
  - "Uncited tax rates, shares, dollar amounts, or claims about where every tax dollar goes."
  - "Examples that imply a city controls county, school district, state, or federal revenue."
  - "A single city presented as representative of all U.S. local governments."
  - "Partisan or adversarial examples framed as proof of wrongdoing."
```
